# Academic Editing and Portuguese to English Translation

Gutemberg Rapôso | Demonstration portfolio sample

## Scope and disclosure

This original, fictional demonstration was prepared with AI assistance for portfolio use. It contains no client material, actual research findings, participant data, or measured outcomes. It is not a selection test or evidence of unaided performance. Both the source passages and the revisions were created for this exercise.

The sample illustrates academic editing and Portuguese-to-English translation for an international education audience. The editorial aim is to improve clarity and consistency while preserving the intended research question and keeping claims within the limits of a proposed study.

## Editing brief

Genre: abstract for a hypothetical research proposal.

Topic: how adult learners interpret written feedback in an online English course.

Editorial boundaries: use consistent US English; distinguish planned methods from findings; do not add a sample size, instrument, citation, or result. Resolve language problems directly, but flag substantive changes for author review.

## Before editing

The present research intends to make an investigation about how written feedbacks are understood by adult students in an online English course. The purpose is understanding the perceptions of the students and how feedback makes them improve their writing. For this, interviews and texts produced by the students will be utilized, and they will be analyzed by themes. The analysis will compare the comments made by the teacher with the changes in the texts. It is expected to prove that clear feedback guarantees better writing and more autonomy. The study will also discuss the difficulties of understanding the comments and the importance of the context. This investigation can be useful for teachers who want to improve their practices in online education.

## After editing

This proposed study will explore how adult learners interpret written feedback in an online English course. It will examine learners’ perceptions of feedback and how they use it when revising their writing. Interviews and learner-produced texts will be analyzed thematically. The analysis will compare teacher comments with revisions to the texts and explore difficulties learners encounter when interpreting feedback. It will also consider the role of the course context in these interpretations. Rather than assuming that feedback improves writing or promotes learner autonomy, the study will examine how learners describe and respond to it. The proposed research may inform teachers’ approaches to written feedback in online language education.

## Six editorial decisions

1. Concision and idiomatic phrasing. “Intends to make an investigation about” becomes “will explore.” The revised opening states the purpose directly without changing the planned status of the work.

2. Grammar and terminology. “Written feedbacks” becomes “written feedback.” Feedback is normally uncountable in this usage. “Adult learners” is used consistently for participants, while “teacher comments” identifies the specific written material being compared.

3. Precision of the research question. “How feedback makes them improve” becomes “how they use it when revising.” Revision is observable in the proposed materials; improvement requires defined assessment criteria. This is a substantive clarification, not merely a grammar correction.

4. Clear reference. The ambiguous pronoun in “they will be analyzed by themes” is replaced with an explicit reference to interviews and texts. “Analyzed thematically” improves phrasing without inventing a coding procedure or analytical framework.

5. Appropriate strength of claims. “Prove” and “guarantees” are removed. The proposed methods do not, as described, establish that feedback causes better writing or greater autonomy. The revised sentence makes this limitation visible instead of presenting an expected result as established fact.

6. Coherence and academic register. The sequence becomes purpose, materials, analysis, interpretive context, and possible relevance. “May inform” replaces a broad usefulness claim while retaining the intended educational application.

Author review required: Decisions 3 and 5 change the framing of the claims and would need approval in a real assignment. The author should also clarify how interviews and textual revisions will be analyzed together, what “course context” includes, and whether writing quality or autonomy will be assessed. Missing methodological details should be requested, not invented.

## Portuguese to English translation

### Fictional Portuguese source

A proposta busca compreender como estudantes adultos interpretam os comentários escritos do professor e os mobilizam na revisão de seus textos. A análise considerará as percepções dos participantes e as alterações entre versões, sem pressupor que toda mudança represente melhora na escrita. Os resultados poderão subsidiar a discussão sobre práticas de devolutiva em cursos de inglês on-line, respeitadas as limitações do contexto investigado.

### English translation

The proposal aims to understand how adult learners interpret their teacher’s written comments and use them to revise their texts. The analysis will consider participants’ perceptions and changes between drafts, without assuming that every change represents an improvement in writing. The findings may inform discussion of feedback practices in online English courses, subject to the limitations of the context examined.

### Translation decisions

- “Mobilizam” becomes “use,” rather than the literal “mobilize,” to express the intended action in natural academic English.
- “Versões” becomes “drafts” because the passage concerns stages of writing, not software versions or separate editions.
- “Subsidiar” becomes “inform,” and “poderão” remains “may.” The translation preserves the conditional contribution rather than implying proof or a guaranteed practical benefit.
